# Voice and tone

**We're the people this happened to.** We talk like a friend who has been through it, is still angry about it, and is now standing next to you saying exactly what to do. We say "we" and "us". We're angry at thieves, at slow operators and at phone makers. We're never angry at you.

The checklist is the calm part. Anger goes into advocacy, never into instructions.

## Rules

1. **Verb first.** "Lock your phone." Not "Your phone should be locked."
2. **One action per line.** If a step has "and", split it.
3. **Say it like it is.** Short words. No softening ("you may want to consider"), no hype ("powerful", "seamless").
4. **We / us for who we are, you for what to do.** "It happened to us. Lock your phone now."
5. **Numbers, not adjectives.** "About 2 minutes." "6 steps." "*#06#."
6. **Angry at institutions, by name of role.** Say "operators", "phone makers", "banks". Name real companies only in sourced advocacy, never in the guide.
7. **No exclamation marks** in layers 1–3. Advocacy can have one, rarely.
8. **No taglines, no wordplay.** If a line sounds like an ad, cut it.
9. **Write for translation.** No idioms, slang or phrasal verbs that machine translation breaks. Mild words like "pissed off" stay out of the product; the attitude comes from bluntness, not swearing.
10. **Sentence case on screens.** Caps only in the condensed notice style and typed labels, and only in scripts that have case.

## By layer

| Layer | Tone | Example lines |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Checklist | Calm, commanding | "Phone stolen? Do this now." · "Lock your phone." · "Block your SIM card. Call your mobile operator from any phone." · "Change your email password first. Email can reset everything else." · "Done. Next: freeze your bank cards." · "All 6 done. Breathe." |
| 2 · Rights & recovery | Firm: you're owed this | "The police have to take your report." · "Don't leave without a copy and a reference number." · "Your operator can block the phone by its IMEI. Ask. If they stall, ask again." · "Your bank should refund fraud you reported quickly. Get it in writing." |
| 3 · Prevention | Practical, a bit warmer | "Do this tonight. It takes 5 minutes." · "Set a SIM PIN. Then a thief can't get your login codes." · "Write your recovery codes on paper. Keep them at home." · "So the next thief gets less." |
| 4 · Advocacy | Angry, collective | "This happened to us. It shouldn't take a website to fix it." · "They blocked my SIM in 4 hours. The thief emptied my account in 40 minutes." · "A blocked phone should be a dead phone. Everywhere." · "Phone makers: stop making stolen phones worth reselling." · "Operators: one call, one minute, blocked." |
| 5 · Service opt-in | Quiet, transparent | "Help the next person this happens to?" · "We want numbers to push operators and phone makers. Both switches are off." · "No name, no phone number, no exact location." · "No thanks" (same button as "Save choice") |

## About-page voice

> Our phones were stolen. We spent the next hour on hold, searching on borrowed phones, and finding out too late what we should have done first. So we wrote it down. Here's what to do, in order. It's free. It always will be.

## Use and avoid

| Use | Avoid | Why |
|---|---|---|
| stolen, taken | "lost your phone" (unless it was) | Accuracy. Don't imply carelessness. |
| It happened to us | "We understand how you feel" | Solidarity, not scripted empathy. |
| Lock, block, change, report | secure, safeguard, mitigate, empower | Concrete verbs act and translate. |
| about 2 minutes | quickly, ASAP, immediately!!! | Facts calm people; pressure panics them. |
| operators, phone makers | "the industry", "stakeholders" | Point at who can fix it. |
| thieves (advocacy) | "criminals are draining your account right now" | Angry, never fear-mongering. |
| optional, off until you switch it on | free trial, limited time, act now | Never cash in on panic. |
| happens to anyone | you should have, careless, unfortunately | No victim-blaming. |

## Accessibility of language

- Aim for a reading age of 11–12.
- Don't use direction words ("on the right"); they flip in RTL. Name the button by its label.
- Give every pictogram-only control an accessible name in the current language.
